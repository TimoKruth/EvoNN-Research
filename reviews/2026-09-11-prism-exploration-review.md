# Prism review — 2026-09-11

**Recommendation:** improve Prism's ability to develop and revisit alternatives while continuing to exploit strong discoveries. Preserve all existing architecture families. Make resource allocation revisable, protect exploration from short-term rankings, and extend what the genome can express. Current results justify further investment in Prism; they do not justify eliminating apparently weak approaches.

This is a review and proposal, not an adopted execution plan or a new campaign. The authoritative next steps remain in [CONSOLIDATED_PLAN.md](../CONSOLIDATED_PLAN.md). No engine defaults, historical results or protected test data were changed or accessed.

**Evidence reviewed.** The latest completed campaign ran September 10 and was analyzed September 11: producer `e1d005a6cfe93056bbd033209130ef2c0197db52`, Tier B, seeds 53–60, budgets 128/256, all four engines plus Contenders. Prism contributes 16 runs and 3,072 successful fits, with no failed or invalid fits. These are total pack budgets: only 32/64 fits per task per run. The [frozen result receipt](../governance/all-engines-high-budget-results-20260911.json) contains the official inference and earlier verification. This review additionally checked 32 attempt/telemetry files against export-manifest hashes, reconciled all Prism attempt winners with the receipt-bound descriptive data, and confirmed that the five reviewed Prism source files match the frozen producer byte for byte. See the [reproducible audit script](../.artifacts/prism-review-20260911/audit.py) and [derived measurements](../.artifacts/prism-review-20260911/audit.json). This was not a fresh full export validation or model replay; those are recorded in the prior receipt.

**1. Highest priority: make diversity available for reproduction.** [search.py](../EvoNN-Prism/src/prism/search.py) saves family niches and a quality/parameter Pareto archive, but `_reproduce` selects parents only from the just-evaluated population. With population four and tournaments of three, strictly third- and fourth-ranked candidates have zero chance of becoming tournament parents. Ties alter this statement. The forced alternative-family slot preserves recurring exposure, but it is derived from tournament-selected parents, not necessarily from the archived best member of that family. Occupying an archive therefore does not ensure that a promising alternative gets descendants.

At 256, the LM family allocation across eight runs is: embedding 253/512 fits; causal Transformer 93; gated state space 53; attention 50; sparse state space 23; sparse attention 21; ordinary state space 19. All seven families were tried, but exposure differs substantially. This alone does not establish that an allocation is wrong. More revealingly, embedding wins 6/8 LM runs at 128 and 4/8 at 256; the higher-budget winners also include gated state space twice, causal Transformer once and attention once. Digits gains a convolutional winner at 256. These observations argue against pruning families based on the shorter runs.

Propose mixed parent selection from current high performers, archived alternatives, and revisited or fresh proposals. Give each supported family a positive recurring opportunity, and let some lineages reproduce through several initially weak steps. Within-family competition can help, but also retain a bounded reservoir of young, unusual or uncertain candidates: keeping only the best member of each family still loses stepping stones. Use behavioral descriptors alongside architecture descriptors and preserve a route outside fixed archive bins. Archive eviction should remove an active residency, not prohibit rediscovery; retain compact provenance and reusable seeds where practical.

The research basis is that [MAP-Elites](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909) maintains diverse high-performing solutions, while [Monte Carlo Elites](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08781) investigates selecting parents from underexplored archive regions. Applying these ideas to Prism is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated Prism improvement. Neither a fixed descriptor map nor an uncertainty score should become the sole admission gate.

**2. Highest priority: distinguish inherited training from architectural merit.** In [run.py](../EvoNN-Prism/src/prism/run.py), the first generation gets six epochs; subsequent full allocations are twelve. Exact inheritance receives 30% of that allocation and partial inheritance 60%, rounded up. At 256, 1,948/2,048 fits inherit weights: 592 exact and 1,356 partial. Among partial transfers, the copied parameter fraction ranges from 0.37% to 100%; 208 copy less than one quarter. They receive the same fractional epoch allowance despite very different transfer coverage. Parameter coverage also cannot establish functional usefulness.

Propose allocation informed by transferred components, learning progress and uncertainty, with an explicit route to a full training allowance even after unsuccessful inheritance. Reserve bounded reevaluation opportunities for slow or unusual candidates independent of their early rank. Record per-epoch curves and cumulative lineage updates. Compare selected architectures both with inherited weights and from fresh initialization under matched training allowances; keep warm-start performance as a useful separate outcome. Repeated genome fits can be valuable continuation, so do not discard them as duplicate evaluations. In this cohort, 627/2,048 fits revisit a genome within its own run/task. Their cost and benefit should be reported separately from new-genome discovery.

The optimizer moments and schedule currently restart on every fit in [training.py](../EvoNN-Prism/src/prism/training.py). Test resumable optimizer state for exact continuation separately from deliberate restarts; do not transplant moments indiscriminately across changed structures. Exact continuation should also compare against its starting checkpoint: `best_loss` currently starts at infinity, so the fit does not include the inherited model before the first update among its candidate checkpoints. The overall exported historical winner is retained independently, but the active weight cache may still accept a weaker continuation.

**3. High priority: make the proposal space genuinely extensible.** [genome.py](../EvoNN-Prism/src/prism/genome.py) contains a fixed family catalog and one family choice for the entire model. Some validated settings are much broader than ordinary mutations: width accepts 4–256 but ordinary width mutation draws only 8/16/32/64; embedding dimension accepts 4–256 but its mutation draws 16/32/64. Depth mostly grows until six and then shrinks once. Uniform crossover does not exchange the attention-specific configuration fields. Thus schema support does not imply effective search access.

Preserve existing small proposals while adding reversible grow/shrink operations, occasional large jumps, fresh seeds, and proposal support throughout the admitted parameter ranges. Test reachability and operator applicability without training. Record actual changes, including fallback mutations and forced family replacements; a genome hash change need not mean a different computed function. Condition crossover adaptation on task/family context, with uncertainty and a positive exploration floor. The current crossover success statistic is shared across benchmarks; it should not turn failure in one context into a general ban.

For longer-term breadth, introduce a versioned composition grammar: compatible attention, recurrence, convolution, gating, sparse and dense blocks can coexist, with add/remove/rewire mutations. Keep today's whole-family models as valid constructors. Provide a documented operator/compiler extension boundary so previously unrepresented mechanisms can be added without redesigning selection. Require valid shapes, causal semantics where needed, reproducibility and explicit resource accounting. Keep runtime limits as separately qualified operational envelopes rather than evidence that larger or slower ideas are scientifically uninteresting. Any envelope extension needs its own qualification; this proposal does not raise existing limits.

**4. High priority: broaden the evidence that guides search.** The frozen LM workload has 16-byte contexts, 1,536 training contexts and 384 validation contexts. Prism's embedding family is pointwise in the compiler: each token's output has no dependency on earlier tokens. Its strong results demonstrate that this bounded task can reward local conditional modeling. They do not show that context-sensitive architectures lack value.

Retain this benchmark and add separately versioned workloads with longer dependencies, more data, different corpora and distribution shifts, plus harder image and tabular tasks. Include simple local-context baselines and controlled context-removal diagnostics. Select and report a diverse shortlist using training/validation data, then use a frozen independent assessment. Do not repeatedly consult the same protected test for policy development. Broader coverage and explicit per-task outcomes should complement aggregate quality, so an LM gain cannot hide an image regression.

**5. Supporting priority: recover runtime without deleting scientific options.** At 256, measured fit training accounts for approximately 29–54% of each Prism run's elapsed time. The remainder includes orchestration, compilation, process startup, serialization, checkpointing and export work; this review does not isolate those components or establish that all are avoidable. [training.py](../EvoNN-Prism/src/prism/training.py) and [tensors.py](../EvoNN-Prism/src/prism/tensors.py) also move weights/gradients through NumPy each optimizer step. These runs used native MLX on CPU, not GPU.

Profile phases before optimizing. Candidates include bounded worker reuse, binary array transport, and an optional backend-resident optimizer with parity checks. Preserve timeout isolation, numerical checks, accounting and deterministic resume. Report both quality at equal fit counts and quality at equal wall time. A cheaper evaluator should buy more exploration; it should not be used to redefine expensive families out of the search space.

**Suggested validation order.** First add lineage, parent-selection, mutation-effect, per-epoch and phase-timing observability. Then test archive-parent access plus protected exploration against unchanged Prism. Separately test inheritance/training allocation, then proposal reachability and compositional extensions. Each comparison must include unchanged Prism, the declared Prism variant(s), Topograph, Stratograph, Primordia and Contenders on every declared benchmark/budget/seed combination. Freeze fresh seeds, budgets, training/work accounting, quality margins, diversity measures and multiplicity treatment before execution. Missing or failed required engines leave the comparison incomplete. Retain existing bounded runtime limits and historical evidence.

Acceptance should combine per-task quality and uncertainty, cost, effective structural/behavioral diversity, family exposure, sustained lineage development, and successful revisits after early weakness. Neither raw archive occupancy nor more unique hashes suffices. Preserve variants with documented tradeoffs even when they do not become defaults; do not redefine an inconclusive short-budget result as permanent rejection.

No finite computation can explore every possibility or guarantee the globally best solution. A practical commitment is achievable: current low scores never become permanent architectural exclusions, alternatives retain meaningful recurring opportunities, and the representation remains open to new mechanisms.
